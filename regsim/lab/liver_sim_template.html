Liver Deformation Lab

Work on the left like the surgeon: Press dents the surface, Grasp clips a point so you can pull it or lift it. The right shows the whole liver deforming the same way. Physics: LIBR, a linear-elastic finite-element model of this liver; every tool target is met with the least strain energy, so the organ always deforms as one elastic body.

LIBR is linear elasticity: right for dents, gas and sag. A press = a local dent (contact solution for the tool tip pressing an elastic body) plus organ-scale movement (LIBR), split by their stiffness. A big lift that should rotate a lobe about its attachment will instead stretch it; the page warns when the surface is stretched beyond ~20 %. Gas and gravity sizes are placeholders (gas 100 % → 10 mm, head-up 30° → 20 mm) until checked against measured deformations. Tool targets do not depend on stiffness: when a tool forces a point to move, an elastic body's shape does not depend on how stiff it is, only the force does.
