01

What the method gets

Photo, mask, camera, CT, depth shape

The amber line is the liver mask: the pixels that show liver. The tinted 3D shape is the CT liver, drawn through the camera.

02

Use the real camera

Same lens as the photo, or the size lies

The CT is drawn through a virtual camera with the photo's focal length and centre. A shorter focal length (wider lens) makes the same liver look smaller, so the "right size" would appear at the wrong distance.

×1.00

Try it: set ×0.7, press Auto size & centre (step 4), and watch "CT distance" and "Distance off" in the score.

03

Find the rough pose

Which side faces the camera, where the head is

This is the hard step. Very different poses can give the same outline, and the polish in step 6 only fixes small errors. Three ways to get close:

a. A hint, like the VLM would give

Head points

b. Try many starts

768 cameras all around the liver (96 directions × 8 roll angles), each sized and centred on the mask, then scored with J.

c. By hand

Rotate tool: drag to tilt the liver. Spin tool: drag left/right to turn it in the picture.

04

Size and position

The distance follows from lens + CT size

A liver of known size, seen through a known lens, has the right size in the picture at only one distance. So once the rotation is roughly right, the distance and the sideways position can be read off the mask.

By hand: scroll on the picture (or the Distance tool) moves the CT along the line of sight; the Move tool shifts it sideways.

05

Score the pose

Outline gap A and shape mismatch B

A: how far apart the two outlines are, in pixels of the full 1920-wide image (mask to CT plus CT to mask). B: how much the tilt of the CT surface disagrees with the tilt in the depth map (0 = same, 0.02 ≈ 11°). The shape term is what separates poses with the same outline.

Switch the view to Shape error to see where the tilts disagree. On the synthetic scene, compare the perfect depth map with the distorted ones from our shape-trust test.

06

Polish

Small automatic nudges that lower J

A local search (Nelder–Mead, the same settings as our tests: first steps of 10° and 20 mm, at most 300 tries) moves the pose in 3 rotations and 3 shifts and keeps whatever lowers J. It cannot jump from a wrong pose to a far-away right one.

07

Bend the liver

Gas and tools push the liver out of its CT shape

LIBR describes the bend as a few pushes at control points on the liver, each spreading through the tissue. Choose the Bend tool and drag a dot: the tissue around it follows.

40 mm

Simplified: each push spreads as a smooth bump. Real LIBR uses precomputed elastic responses of the CT liver, adds a penalty for strain energy, and fits the pushes automatically.
